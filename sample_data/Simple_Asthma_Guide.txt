Simple Health Guide: Asthma Overview & Management

What is Asthma?
Asthma is a common long-term condition that affects the airways in the lungs. It causes the airways to become inflamed, swollen, and narrow, making it harder to breathe.

Common Symptoms:
- Wheezing (a whistling sound when breathing)
- Shortness of breath
- Tightness in the chest
- Persistent coughing, especially at night or early morning

Common Triggers:
1. Airborne allergens (pollen, dust mites, pet dander, mold)
2. Cold air and sudden temperature changes
3. Physical exercise
4. Respiratory infections (such as colds or the flu)
5. Smoke, pollution, and strong chemical fumes

Treatment and Inhalers:
- Reliever Inhalers (usually blue, e.g., Albuterol): Used quickly during an asthma attack to open the airways within minutes.
- Preventer/Controller Inhalers (usually brown or orange, e.g., Fluticasone): Taken every day to reduce inflammation and prevent symptoms from starting.

When to Seek Emergency Care:
If a reliever inhaler does not help after 10 puffs, or if the person is struggling to speak in full sentences, call emergency medical services immediately.
