Test Medical Document — Hypertension

Hypertension is a chronic medical condition characterized by persistently elevated blood pressure and is an important risk factor for cardiovascular disease, stroke, chronic kidney disease, and other complications. Blood pressure is influenced by genetic, environmental, and lifestyle factors. Modifiable risk factors include high dietary sodium intake, excessive alcohol consumption, physical inactivity, obesity, and diets low in fruits and vegetables. Regular physical activity, maintaining a healthy body weight, reducing sodium intake, limiting alcohol consumption, and following a balanced diet can contribute to improved blood pressure control. Hypertension is often asymptomatic, which is why regular blood pressure measurement is important for early detection. Management generally involves lifestyle modification and, when appropriate, antihypertensive medication. Common classes of antihypertensive medications include ACE inhibitors, angiotensin II receptor blockers, calcium channel blockers, and thiazide-type diuretics. The choice of treatment can depend on factors such as the patient's overall health, age, kidney function, cardiovascular risk, and other medical conditions. Long-term blood pressure control can reduce the risk of cardiovascular and kidney-related complications.
